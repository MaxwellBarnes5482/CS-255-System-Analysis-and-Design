# CS 255 – System Analysis and Design

## DriverPass Project Reflection

**Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?**

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare for their driving tests. They needed an online system where customers could create accounts, purchase training packages, schedule driving lessons, and take practice tests. The system also needed to help employees manage appointments, instructors, vehicles, and customer information. My responsibility was to analyze the company's needs and design a system that would support its daily operations.

**What did you do particularly well?**

I think one thing I did particularly well was breaking down the system's requirements into diagrams that showed how different parts of DriverPass would work together. Creating the activity diagrams helped me understand the steps involved in scheduling driving lessons and taking practice tests. I also think I did a good job considering how the system would be used by different people, including customers, instructors, and office employees.

**If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?**

If I could revise one part of my work, I would focus on improving the business requirements document from Project One. Looking back, I think I could have made some of the requirements more specific and organized them better. I would also make sure each requirement clearly explains what the system needs to accomplish. This would make it easier for developers to understand the expectations and use the document during development.

**How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?**

I interpreted the user's needs by reviewing the DriverPass interview and identifying the main features the company wanted. I used that information to create diagrams showing how customers would schedule lessons, take practice tests, and interact with the system. I also considered the responsibilities of employees and the importance of protecting customer information. Understanding the user's needs is important because a system might work correctly from a technical standpoint but still fail if it doesn't solve the problems the customer actually has.

**How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?**

When designing software, I think it's important to understand the problem before deciding how the system should work. I would start by gathering requirements, identifying the different users, and determining what tasks the system needs to support. From there, I would use tools like UML diagrams to organize the design and show how different features connect. In the future, I would also focus on reviewing requirements with the client throughout the process so misunderstandings can be corrected before development begins.
